# End-to-End Workflow

Read this file when the user requests a complete proposal process or when work spans more than one gate.

## Phase 0: Intake and constraint map

### Goal

Define what counts as an acceptable proposal before generating recommendations.

### Inputs

- degree and discipline;
- advisor comments and preferences;
- school template and model proposals;
- deadline and expected length;
- accessible databases and company materials;
- prohibited methods, duplicate cases, and excluded topics;
- requested deliverables and file formats.

### Actions

1. Separate hard constraints from preferences.
2. Translate advisor language into decision criteria.
3. Record ambiguous decisions that require confirmation.
4. Define the evidence standard appropriate to the degree.

### Output

A compact constraint map used in all later scoring and revisions.

### Quality checkpoint

The assistant can explain why a topic would be accepted or rejected without relying on personal taste.

## Phase 1: Candidate generation and convergence

### Goal

Produce a small, comparable candidate pool rather than prematurely committing to one case.

### Actions

1. Generate candidates from research problems, institutional changes, accounting treatments, governance mechanisms, or observable contradictions.
2. Attach a candidate case only after defining the problem.
3. Score all candidates with one rubric.
4. Remove cases that conflict with group restrictions or lack a plausible evidence path.

### Output

A candidate library containing title, central problem, case, evidence entry points, risks, score, and status.

### Quality checkpoint

At least three candidates can be compared on the same dimensions. A high score does not automatically mean “ready to write.”

When candidates have already been rejected, classify the rejection before generating replacements. Use [topic-selection-lessons.md](topic-selection-lessons.md) to decide whether to reject the evidence base, narrow the question, or retain the original issue as one module in a broader study.

## Phase 2: P0 screening

### Goal

Decide whether a candidate deserves deeper investigation.

### Actions

Check importance, disciplinary fit, case distinctiveness, policy or practice relevance, preliminary evidence access, and duplication risk.

### Output

Pass, conditional pass, or fail, plus the exact questions to test in P1.

### Quality checkpoint

The candidate contains an explicit research tension, not merely a topical company or policy keyword.

## Phase 3: P1 evidence validation

### Goal

Determine whether available evidence can support the intended paper.

### Actions

1. Build a dated event or governance timeline.
2. Collect official and primary sources.
3. Build a multi-year data table and document all definitions.
4. Conduct a pilot content code, calculation, comparison, or document trace.
5. Map each intended claim to available evidence.
6. Verify core literature and identify unsupported areas.

### Output

An evidence matrix, pilot result, source inventory, and risk-adjusted P1 decision.

### Quality checkpoint

At least one meaningful analytical operation has been completed. The proposal does not depend on inaccessible internal data.

## Phase 4: Research question design

### Goal

Create one coherent analytical chain.

### Actions

1. State the central puzzle in one sentence.
2. Choose a mechanism or evaluation structure.
3. Write one main question.
4. Write three or four subquestions covering motivation, internalization, operating mechanism, and result or boundary.
5. Map each subquestion to a chapter, method, and evidence source.

### Output

A question tree and chapter-evidence map.

### Quality checkpoint

Removing any subquestion would leave a visible gap; adding another would broaden the study beyond its central mechanism.

## Phase 5: Literature review and theory

### Goal

Show what is known, where findings differ, and why the proposed question remains useful.

### Actions

1. Group literature by research stream or debate.
2. Distinguish foundational, current, contextual, and method sources.
3. Summarize positions and evidence, not titles.
4. End each stream with its limitation relative to the research question.
5. Select theories only when they explain a part of the mechanism.

### Output

A literature matrix, verified reference library, literature review, and theory-to-question map.

### Quality checkpoint

The claimed gap is supported by the reviewed literature and does not rely on “no one studied this company.”

## Phase 6: Method and research design

### Goal

Choose the smallest sufficient set of methods.

### Actions

1. Map each method to a subquestion.
2. Specify data, units of analysis, coding or calculation procedure, and validation method.
3. Separate research methods from analytical operations.
4. Define the observation period and inclusion rules.
5. State evidence limitations without turning the proposal into a defensive disclaimer.

### Output

A method table, coding framework or data dictionary, and research design narrative.

### Quality checkpoint

Each method is executable with accessible data and contributes a necessary analytical capability.

## Phase 7: Proposal drafting

### Goal

Produce a school-compliant, evidence-backed document.

### Suggested drafting order

1. Main question and subquestions.
2. Research design and evidence.
3. Chapter structure.
4. Case background and key data tables.
5. Literature review and theory.
6. Significance and contribution.
7. Introduction, schedule, and references.

Drafting in this order reduces the risk that significance and novelty become detached from the executable design.

### Output

A versioned proposal with editable supporting tables and figures.

### Quality checkpoint

The document can be summarized as one problem, one mechanism, one evidence plan, and one bounded contribution.

## Phase 8: Advisor-feedback correction

### Goal

Change the underlying research design when feedback targets scope or importance, rather than only changing wording.

### Actions

1. Classify feedback as topic, scope, importance, evidence, method, format, or wording.
2. Identify the decision it implies.
3. Revise the smallest set of linked sections needed for consistency.
4. Re-run P0 or P1 if the case, question, or evidence changes materially.
5. Preserve the previous version.

### Output

A new version and a concise change log.

### Quality checkpoint

The revision resolves the advisor's underlying concern and does not merely make the title longer.

## Phase 9: Submission and defense preparation

### Goal

Ensure the proposal is traceable, defensible, and ready for review.

### Actions

1. Run the final content, evidence, citation, method, and layout checks.
2. Prepare answers to importance, novelty, feasibility, method, evidence, and boundary questions.
3. Record the exact file path and version.
4. Keep editable sources for figures and tables.

### Output

Submission copy, editable source files, evidence index, and defense question set.

### Quality checkpoint

Every major claim can be traced to evidence or clearly identified as an interpretation.

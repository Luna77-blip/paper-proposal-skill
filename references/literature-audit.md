# Literature Authenticity and Review Workflow

Read this file before expanding a literature review, claiming a gap, or organizing a reference library.

## Source-status model

| Status | Evidence available | Use rule |
|---|---|---|
| A | Bibliographic record verified; full text obtained; cited claim checked | Safe to cite |
| B | Bibliographic record verified; no legal full text; abstract or authoritative record supports limited claim | Cite cautiously and only within verified scope |
| C | Search result, secondary citation, or title only | Do not cite as evidence yet |
| D | Author, title, year, venue, DOI, or claimed conclusion cannot be verified | Remove |

Do not equate the number of references in a proposal with the number of obtained PDFs. Track both separately.

## Verification sequence

1. Verify title, authors, year, venue, volume, issue, pages, DOI, and document type.
2. Prefer publisher, DOI registry, official repository, institutional library, or established academic database records.
3. Obtain a lawful full text when available.
4. Locate the exact passage, model, result, or definition supporting the intended sentence.
5. Record the source status and local file path.
6. Remove or rewrite sentences whose supporting source cannot be verified.

## Review structure

Organize the review by debate or mechanism rather than by author sequence.

For each stream:

1. define the research focus;
2. explain the main positions;
3. compare evidence and methods;
4. identify disagreement or boundary conditions;
5. state the limitation relative to the proposed question;
6. connect the limitation to the next stream or research design.

## Gap tests

A defensible research gap may concern:

- fragmented theories that have not been connected in one mechanism;
- a new institutional setting that changes assumptions;
- longitudinal evidence where prior work is cross-sectional;
- a measurement, boundary, or data-governance change hidden by aggregate metrics;
- a case type that reveals a theoretically meaningful boundary condition;
- a difference between formal disclosure and operating control.

Weak gaps include:

- “few studies mention this company”;
- “the policy is recent” without a research problem;
- “existing research is insufficient” without showing how;
- “this paper uses multiple methods”;
- “this case is representative” without selection logic.

## Reference-manager organization

Recommended collections:

1. foundational theory;
2. disciplinary core;
3. case and industry;
4. policy, standards, and regulation;
5. research methods;
6. verified but no full text;
7. pending verification;
8. excluded sources.

Use stable filenames such as:

`[stream-code]_[year]_[first-author]_[short-title].pdf`

Keep citation metadata in the reference manager rather than relying on filenames as the authoritative record.

## Current-source warning

Regulation, standards, company leadership, financial data, and recent publications are time-sensitive. Recheck authoritative sources before submission. A source that was accurate during topic selection may be outdated by the defense date.
